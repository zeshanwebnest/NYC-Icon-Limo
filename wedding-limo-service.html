Who Needs a Car

Four sets of people, one plan

Most wedding transport goes wrong in the gaps between these, not within them. We quote the whole day rather than the obvious parts of it.

01

The Couple

To the ceremony unhurried, on to photographs, then to the reception — and away at the end of it without a scramble for a car.

02

The Wedding Party

Everyone in one vehicle, leaving from one address, arriving together. Fewer cars means fewer things to go wrong at eleven in the morning.

03

Parents & Family

Grandparents who should not be finding their own way, and the relatives arriving from three different hotels at three different times.

04

Guest Shuttles

Hotel to ceremony, ceremony to reception, reception to hotel — on a published schedule, so nobody is deciding on the night.

The Day

A timeline, not a pickup list

Most wedding transport plans fail in the same two places: the photograph stop nobody budgeted for, and the gap between ceremony and reception. Both are built in from the start.

01

Getting ready

Collections from however many addresses the morning is spread across, timed backwards from when you need to be at the ceremony.

02

To the ceremony

Arriving with margin rather than exactly on the hour, at the entrance the venue actually wants cars to use.

03

Photographs

The stop that always overruns. We budget real time for it — a park, a street, a rooftop — and the chauffeur waits rather than circling.

04

Ceremony to reception

Your car and the guest shuttles leaving in the right order, so the room fills before you walk into it.

05

The send-off

A car waiting at the end, however late that turns out to be, plus late shuttles back for guests who stayed to the last song.

Booking

When to book, and what we need

Wedding dates are the one thing we cannot create more of. Peak Saturdays between May and October go first, and they go early.

  • Six to twelve months ahead for a peak Saturday — earlier if the date is a long weekend
  • Thirty days is our practical minimum for a multi-vehicle day
  • Weekday and off-season weddings can often be arranged with much less notice
  • See the car before you commit — viewings by appointment, and we would rather you did
  • A written quote per vehicle, with hours, stops and tolls itemised
  • Final timeline confirmed with you about two weeks out, once the venue times are fixed

We cover all five boroughs, Long Island, Westchester, the Hudson Valley, New Jersey and Connecticut — and we will meet out-of-town guests at LaGuardia, JFK or Newark on the same account.
