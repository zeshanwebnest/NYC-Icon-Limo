How much time to leave for JFK, LaGuardia and Newark

September 9, 2026 9 min read Dispatch Desk

Article

Every week someone tells us their last car service picked them up ninety minutes before an international departure from JFK, and every week we wince. The honest answer to “when should the car come?” is that it depends on four things: which airport, which terminal, what hour, and whether you are crossing water. Here is the timing our dispatch desk actually works from.

None of this is a secret formula. It is the accumulated result of running the same trips several thousand times a year and noting where the surprises come from. Use it to sanity-check whatever pickup time you have been quoted — by us or by anyone else.

Start from the gate, not from the door

The common mistake is to plan forward from your front door. Plan backwards from the gate instead, and the answer falls out on its own. Work through five blocks of time in this order:

  1. Boarding closes — typically 15 minutes before departure for domestic, 30 for international. This, not departure time, is your real deadline.
  2. Walk to the gate — 10 minutes in a compact terminal, up to 25 at JFK Terminal 4, where the gate can be a long way from security.
  3. Security — 15 minutes with PreCheck on a quiet morning, 45 or more in a general line during a bank of departures.
  4. Check-in and bag drop — 0 if you are travelling with carry-on only and a mobile boarding pass, 30 if you have bags and no pre-check-in.
  5. The drive — the only part we control, and the part everybody focuses on.

Add those up before you look at a map. For most international departures out of JFK you will land somewhere near three hours before wheels-up, and that number surprises people every time.

The drive itself, by airport

These are the driving allowances we build into a reservation from midtown Manhattan. They already contain a normal amount of slack — they are not best-case times.

Driving time allowances from midtown Manhattan to each New York airport
Airport Off-peak Weekday peak The catch
LaGuardia (LGA) 30–40 min 60–75 min Closest airport, but the Queens–Midtown Tunnel and the Grand Central Parkway both bottleneck at rush hour.
JFK 45–60 min 75–110 min The Van Wyck is the single least predictable stretch of road in our whole operation.
Newark (EWR) 45–60 min 80–120 min The Lincoln Tunnel decides your morning. There is no second option that is reliably faster.

Note how much wider the peak ranges are than the off-peak ones. That spread is the real story: it is not that the drive is slower at 8am, it is that it becomes unpredictable at 8am. A trip that takes 50 minutes on Tuesday can take 95 on Wednesday for no visible reason.

The short version

From midtown, for a domestic departure: leave 3 hours before wheels-up for LGA, 3.5 for JFK or EWR. For an international departure out of JFK, make it 4. If that sounds excessive, it is because the cost of being an hour early is a coffee, and the cost of being ten minutes late is the whole trip.

Which terminal changes the answer

Airports are not single destinations. At JFK, the difference between Terminal 4 and Terminal 8 is a meaningful amount of driving once you are inside the airport perimeter, and at peak times the terminal roadway itself can add fifteen minutes. Tell us the airline when you book, not just the airport — that is the detail that lets dispatch pick the right approach and drop you at the right door.

Newark deserves a specific warning. The newer Terminal A is a genuinely different drive from Terminals B and C, and travellers who last flew from Newark years ago routinely give us the wrong one. Check your boarding pass rather than your memory.

The hours we quietly add time to

Some patterns repeat reliably enough that we build extra margin in without being asked:

  • Thursday and Friday afternoons, roughly 3pm to 7pm. The worst window of the week for every airport, and it starts earlier than most people expect.
  • Sunday evenings, when the returning-weekend traffic and the evening departure bank arrive at the same time.
  • Any day it rains. Rain adds twenty to thirty minutes across the board in this city. It is the most reliable single predictor we have.
  • The first clear morning after bad weather, when rebooked passengers fill the terminals even though the roads look fine.
  • General Assembly week in September, when midtown east of Third Avenue can close with very little notice.

If the flight genuinely cannot be missed, book the car half an hour earlier than the maths says. Nobody has ever called us to complain that they got to the airport with time to spare.

Coming the other way: arrivals

Arrivals work differently, and the good news is that the pressure is off. We track your flight number, so a delay moves your pickup automatically — you do not need to call us from the air, and you are not charged for the hours the aircraft spent circling.

What we do ask you to be realistic about is the gap between landing and reaching the curb. Twenty minutes is fast for a domestic arrival with carry-on only. An international arrival at JFK with checked bags and a passport queue is closer to an hour, sometimes more. We build complimentary wait time into every airport arrival for exactly this reason — the numbers are on our pricing page.

What to tell us when you book

Four details let dispatch do the arithmetic properly, and three of them get left out more often than not:

  • The flight number — so we can track it and adjust without being asked.
  • The airline or terminal — the approach and the drop-off door both depend on it.
  • How many bags, honestly counted. This is what decides whether a sedan is the right car, and it is the detail most often understated.
  • Whether anything after the flight is fixed — a connection, a meeting, a dinner reservation. If there is a hard deadline on the far end, we would rather know now than improvise later.

Give us those and you will get a pickup time with real reasoning behind it, not a round number. If you would like one for a specific flight, send us the details and we will come back with a time and the reasoning, same day.
